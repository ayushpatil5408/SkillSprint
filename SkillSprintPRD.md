# 1. Document Purpose

This document defines the product vision, customer problem, market opportunity, business model, user experience, product requirements, technology requirements, operating model, MVP scope, validation strategy, metrics, roadmap, and long-term platform strategy for **SkillSprint**.

SkillSprint is conceived as an outcome-based career-learning platform that helps college students acquire practical, job-relevant skills through short learning sprints, guided practice, real projects, AI assistance, mentor feedback, practical assessment, and proof-of-work portfolios.

The source case positions SkillSprint around 4–6 week project-based sprints, mentor reviews, and demonstrable portfolio outcomes rather than conventional course completion alone.

This PRD converts that concept into a structured product specification suitable for:

- Startup planning
- Product development
- UX/UI design
- Engineering execution
- Investor discussions
- Business-model validation
- Pilot-program execution
- Future fundraising documentation

# 3. Executive Summary

## 3.1 Product Vision

> **SkillSprint is an outcome-based career-learning platform that helps college students learn job-relevant skills through short, project-based learning sprints, supported by AI and mentors, and finish with demonstrable proof of what they can actually build.**

The core philosophy is:

> **Learn → Practice → Build → Get Feedback → Prove → Showcase**

Instead of primarily competing on the amount of educational content available, SkillSprint aims to compete on the quality and usefulness of the **learning outcome**.

The source concept identifies conventional online learning problems including course abandonment, tutorial overload, lack of practical application, and certificates that do not sufficiently demonstrate practical capability.

---

# 4. The Problem

## 4.1 Core Problem Statement

College students need practical, career-relevant skills alongside their academic education.

However, there is often a gap between:

**Learning something**

and

**being able to demonstrate that you can actually do it.**

A student may:

- Watch educational videos
- Complete quizzes
- Finish a course
- Receive a certificate

while still struggling to answer:

> **“What can you actually build or do with this skill?”**

SkillSprint therefore focuses on converting learning into demonstrable capability.

### Core problem

> **College students need an affordable and achievable way to acquire practical, career-relevant skills and demonstrate those skills through real projects rather than merely accumulating course-completion certificates.**

---

# 5. Why the Problem Matters

The source research references the World Economic Forum's *Future of Jobs Report 2025*, which identifies skills gaps as a significant barrier for employers and highlights changing skill requirements, analytical thinking, technological literacy, AI and big data, creative thinking, resilience, and lifelong learning.

### Product implication

SkillSprint should not optimize simply for:

> “How many courses did the student finish?”

It should optimize for:

> **Technical skill + problem solving + practical application + evidence of ability.**

# 27. Credential Strategy

The platform may provide a credential after successful completion.

However, the credential should be linked to actual evidence.

A SkillSprint credential should ideally answer:

1. What skill was assessed?
2. What project was completed?
3. What capabilities were demonstrated?
4. What assessment was performed?
5. Was mentor verification completed?
6. What evidence is available?

The source uses Microsoft's Applied Skills model as a relevant benchmark for scenario-based practical skill validation.

# 33. Functional Requirements

## FR-01 — User Registration

The system shall allow students to create an account.

## FR-02 — Student Profile

The system shall maintain a student profile containing relevant educational, skill, career, and portfolio information.

## FR-03 — Skill Assessment

The system shall allow users to complete an initial skill assessment.

## FR-04 — Sprint Discovery

Students shall be able to browse and inspect available Sprints.

## FR-05 — Sprint Enrollment

Students shall be able to purchase/enroll in a Sprint.

## FR-06 — Learning Delivery

The system shall provide structured learning modules.

## FR-07 — Practice

The system shall provide practice activities linked to learning objectives.

## FR-08 — Progress

The system shall track learning and Sprint progress.

## FR-09 — Project Submission

Students shall be able to submit project work and supporting evidence.

## FR-10 — AI Assistance

Students shall be able to interact with an AI learning assistant.

## FR-11 — Mentor Review

Mentors shall be able to review submissions and provide structured feedback.

## FR-12 — Assessment

The system shall support final practical assessment.

## FR-13 — Portfolio

Successful students shall receive a project/skill portfolio artifact.

## FR-14 — Credential

The system shall issue a credential when defined completion criteria are met.

## FR-15 — Recommendations

The system should recommend subsequent Sprints based on completed learning and career goals.

## FR-16 — Payments

The system shall support Sprint payment.

## FR-17 — Refunds

The system shall support refund processing according to defined policies.

## FR-18 — Analytics

The system shall track acquisition, activation, engagement, outcomes, and business metrics.

# 64. Ethical AI & Academic Integrity

SkillSprint must preserve authentic student work.

AI should assist learning without manufacturing false evidence.

### AI may:

- Explain
- Coach
- Hint
- Debug
- Question
- Recommend
- Personalize practice

### AI should not:

- Complete assessments
- Generate deceptive credentials
- Falsify project evidence
- Automatically produce entire submissions as if they were independently created by the student

The stronger the proof-of-work system becomes, the more important this distinction becomes.
